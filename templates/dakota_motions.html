⚖️ Motions & Legal Actions

Complete guide to all defensive motions and legal countermeasures

🛡️ Pre-Trial Motions (File BEFORE First Hearing)

1. Motion to Dismiss
When to File: Immediately upon receiving Summons & Complaint if grounds exist
Grounds for Dismissal:
  • Improper Notice: 14-day notice for rent not given, wrong amount stated, or notice period not completed
  • Lack of Standing: Person filing is not actual landlord or authorized agent
  • Jurisdictional Defects: Wrong court, wrong county, or procedural errors
  • Service Issues: Improper service of Summons & Complaint (not personal service or proper substituted service)
  • Failure to State a Claim: Complaint doesn't allege sufficient facts to support eviction
How to File:
  1. Draft Motion to Dismiss with specific grounds cited
  2. Include Memorandum of Law citing MN Stat. § 504B.321
  3. File with court clerk ($310 filing fee or IFP waiver)
  4. Serve copy on landlord's attorney via mail/email
  5. File Affidavit of Service proving you served opposing counsel
📄 Form: Motion to Dismiss + Memorandum of Law
⏰ File within 7-14 days of service or with Answer
2. Motion for Continuance
When to File: As soon as you know you need more time (minimum 5 days before hearing)
Valid Reasons:
  • Need Legal Counsel: You're seeking attorney but haven't secured one yet
  • Medical Emergency: Hospitalization or serious illness prevents attendance
  • Pending Emergency Rental Assistance: Application submitted, awaiting approval
  • Key Witness Unavailable: Essential witness has conflicting obligation
  • Discovery Needed: Need time to gather evidence, request documents
  • Lack of Preparation Time: Served very close to hearing date
Required Elements:
  1. Specific reason for continuance with supporting evidence
  2. Proposed new date (typically 2-4 weeks out)
  3. Statement that you contacted opposing counsel (attempt to agree)
  4. Declaration under penalty of perjury
📄 Form: Motion for Continuance + Supporting Affidavit
⏰ File at least 5 days before scheduled hearing
💡 Strategy Tip: Judges more likely to grant if you have legal aid application pending or rental assistance submitted. Attach proof!
3. Motion for Discovery
When to File: Immediately after Answer if you need landlord's records
What You Can Request:
  • Payment Records: Complete ledger showing all payments, charges, and credits
  • Lease Agreement: Original signed lease and all addendums
  • Repair Requests: All maintenance requests you submitted (emails, work orders)
  • Inspection Reports: City/county housing inspection reports
  • Prior Eviction Cases: Other eviction cases landlord filed (pattern evidence)
  • Property License: Rental license and code compliance records
Discovery Methods:
  • Request for Production of Documents: Formal written request for specific documents
  • Interrogatories: Written questions landlord must answer under oath
  • Request for Admissions: Statements landlord must admit or deny
  • Subpoena Duces Tecum: Court order for third parties (city, utilities)
📄 Forms: Request for Production + Interrogatories + Subpoena
⏰ File ASAP - landlord has 10-20 days to respond
4. Motion to Compel (If Landlord Ignores Discovery)
When to File: After landlord fails to respond to discovery requests within deadline
Requirements:
  1. You properly served discovery requests
  2. Response deadline has passed
  3. You made good faith attempt to obtain responses (email/phone)
  4. You include copies of original discovery and proof of service
Possible Outcomes:
  • Judge orders landlord to produce documents within 5-10 days
  • Adverse inference (court assumes documents would hurt landlord's case)
  • Sanctions against landlord (attorney fees, costs)
  • Case dismissal if discovery is critical and landlord refuses
📄 Form: Motion to Compel + Meet-and-Confer Declaration

⏱️ Emergency Motions (File Urgently When Needed)

5. Motion to Stay Writ of Restitution
When to File: IMMEDIATELY after losing at trial, BEFORE Writ is issued
Automatic Stay (Appeal):
  • File Notice of Appeal within 10 days of judgment
  • Post bond = 3 months' rent (unless waived for poverty)
  • Automatic stay until appeal decided
  • Must pay ongoing rent into court registry
Discretionary Stay (Without Appeal):
  • Pending Rental Assistance: Money coming but not yet received
  • Medical Emergency: Severe illness prevents moving
  • School Year: Children enrolled, mid-school year
  • No Alternative Housing: Homeless shelters full, no family
  • Extreme Weather: Minnesota winter, health risk
📄 Form: Motion to Stay Writ + Supporting Affidavit + Proposed Bond
⚠️ CRITICAL: File before Writ issued (usually 10-14 days after judgment)
💡 Emergency Strategy: If you have rental assistance pending with approval letter, attach it! Judges often grant 30-60 day stays while assistance processes.
6. Emergency Motion for Temporary Restraining Order (TRO)
When to File: When landlord is engaging in illegal lockout or utility shutoff
Grounds for TRO:
  • Illegal Lockout: Landlord changed locks without court order
  • Utility Shutoff: Landlord shut off heat, water, electricity
  • Harassment: Threats, intimidation, property damage
  • Unsafe Conditions: Immediate danger to health/safety
TRO Process (Ex Parte - Without Landlord Present):
  1. File Motion + detailed Affidavit with photos/evidence
  2. Request same-day or next-day hearing
  3. Judge may issue temporary order immediately
  4. Full hearing scheduled within 14 days
📄 Form: Emergency TRO Motion + Detailed Affidavit + Photos
⚠️ File same day as incident - time is critical!

🎯 Trial Motions (File During/After Trial)

7. Motion for Directed Verdict
When to File: After landlord presents their case, before you present yours
Standard: Landlord failed to prove essential element of eviction case
  • No proof of proper notice given
  • No proof you owe rent (no ledger, no receipts)
  • No proof of lease violation occurred
  • No proof of damages or harm
How to Present: Oral motion stating "Plaintiff has failed to meet burden of proof on [specific element]. We move for directed verdict."
💡 Pro Tip: If granted, case dismissed immediately. If denied, you still present your defenses.
8. Motion for New Trial
When to File: Within 15 days of final judgment if serious errors occurred
Grounds:
  • Newly Discovered Evidence: Critical evidence not available at trial
  • Manifest Injustice: Verdict against weight of evidence
  • Jury Misconduct: (If jury trial)
  • Errors of Law: Judge made legal errors
  • Fraud/Perjury: Landlord lied under oath (provable)
📄 Form: Motion for New Trial + Memorandum of Law
⏰ File within 15 days of judgment
9. Motion to Vacate Judgment (Default)
When to File: If default judgment entered because you didn't appear/answer
Requirements for Relief:
  1. Excusable Neglect: Valid reason for not appearing (hospitalization, wrong address, etc.)
  2. Timely Motion: File quickly (within reasonable time, preferably <30 days)
  3. Meritorious Defense: You have valid defenses if case reopened
  4. No Prejudice: Landlord won't be unfairly harmed by vacating
Supporting Evidence:
  • Hospital records, doctor's note
  • Proof of wrong address on service
  • Employer letter (mandatory work, couldn't leave)
  • Proposed Answer with defenses
📄 Form: Motion to Vacate Default + Supporting Affidavit + Proposed Answer
⏰ File ASAP (within 30 days is best, but can be longer with strong grounds)
