📅 Dakota County Eviction Timeline

Day 0: Eviction Notice Served

Landlord must serve proper notice:
• Non-payment of rent: 14-day notice
• Lease violation: 30-day notice (or less if material violation)
• No lease/month-to-month: 1 month notice for month-to-month tenancy

After Notice Period: Summons & Complaint Filed

If you don't move out or cure the violation, landlord files eviction lawsuit (Unlawful Detainer) with Dakota County Court.
Action Required: You will be served with court papers showing your court date.

Within 7-14 Days: File Your Answer

CRITICAL: File written Answer with the court AND serve copy to landlord's attorney.
• Admit or deny each claim
• Raise all defenses
• File fee: $310 (may be waived if low income)

Court Hearing: 7-14 Days After Filing

Eviction hearings in Dakota County are typically scheduled quickly.
• Bring all evidence (receipts, photos, communications)
• Bring witnesses if applicable
• Dress professionally and arrive early

After Judgment: Writ of Recovery

If you lose, landlord can request Writ of Recovery. Sheriff will post 24-hour notice before lockout.
Appeal: Must file within 10 days of judgment.

🛡️ Common Legal Defenses in Dakota County

1. Improper Notice

Landlord must provide proper written notice with correct timeframe. Notice must include specific reasons and comply with MN Stat. § 504B.135.
Example: 14-day notice for rent must state exact amount owed and how calculated.

2. Retaliation (MN Stat. § 504B.285)

Landlord cannot evict in retaliation for:
• Reporting code violations
• Requesting repairs
• Joining tenant organization
• Complaining to government agency
Timeline: Within 90 days of protected activity creates presumption of retaliation.

3. Discrimination

Federal and MN fair housing laws prohibit eviction based on:
• Race, color, national origin
• Religion
• Sex (including sexual harassment)
• Disability
• Familial status (children)
• Source of income (Section 8)

4. Rent Already Paid

Bring proof of payment:
• Bank statements
• Canceled checks
• Money order receipts
• Text/email confirmations
• Venmo/CashApp records

5. Uninhabitable Conditions (Implied Warranty of Habitability)

Landlord must maintain habitable property. Major defects may reduce/eliminate rent owed:
• No heat in winter
• No hot water
• Severe mold or pest infestation
• Broken locks/no security
• Roof leaks causing damage
Evidence: Photos, inspection reports, repair requests.

6. Lease Violation Not True

Deny allegations and provide evidence:
• Witnesses
• Documentation
• Photos/videos
• Communications with landlord

7. Landlord Breach of Lease

Landlord failed to comply with lease terms:
• Did not provide promised services
• Violated quiet enjoyment
• Entered without proper notice
• Failed to maintain property per lease

📖 Key Minnesota Statutes for Dakota County

MN Stat. § 504B.001-504B.471 - Landlord-Tenant Law

Complete Minnesota landlord-tenant statutes covering leases, evictions, deposits, and tenant rights.

MN Stat. § 504B.135 - Security Deposits

• Must be returned within 21 days of move-out
• With itemized list of deductions
• Interest required if deposit > $1,000 and tenancy > 1 year
• Penalty: Tenant may recover deposit plus actual damages or $500, whichever is greater

MN Stat. § 504B.161 - Landlord Entry Rights

Landlord must give reasonable notice (typically 24 hours) except emergencies.
Entry must be at reasonable times and for legitimate purposes.

MN Stat. § 504B.285 - Retaliation Prohibited

Landlord cannot retaliate within 90 days of tenant asserting rights.
Tenant can recover 3 months' rent plus attorney fees if retaliation proven.

MN Stat. § 504B.291 - Residential Tenant's Remedies

Tenant remedies for landlord violations:
• Rent abatement
• Repair and deduct
• Lawsuit for damages
• Termination of lease

MN Stat. § 504B.371 - Eviction Action Procedures

Governs eviction procedures in Minnesota including notice requirements, filing procedures, and hearing process.

💡 Tips for Court Success

  • File your Answer on time - this is critical!
  • Bring all evidence: receipts, photos, emails, texts, repair requests
  • Bring copies for the judge and opposing attorney
  • Dress professionally and arrive 30 minutes early
  • Be respectful and only speak when asked
  • Stick to facts, not emotions or personal attacks
  • Bring witnesses who can testify to conditions or payments
  • If you can't afford the filing fee, ask for a fee waiver (IFP form)
  • Consider requesting mediation to negotiate a settlement
