⚔️ Countersuits & Offensive Legal Actions

Turn defense into offense - Sue your landlord for violations

⚠️ IMPORTANT: You can file a countersuit AS PART OF THE EVICTION CASE or as a separate lawsuit. Filing as a counterclaim in the eviction case is often faster and can offset rent owed.

💰 Top Countersuits & Claims

1. Security Deposit Violation Countersuit
📖 MN Stat. § 504B.178 - Security Deposit Act

When to File: Landlord failed to return your security deposit within 21 days of move-out, or withheld deposit improperly without itemized statement.

💵 DAMAGES AVAILABLE:
  • Full Deposit Back
  • PLUS Penalty: Actual damages OR $500 (whichever is greater)
  • PLUS Interest: If held improperly for over 21 days
  • PLUS Attorney Fees & Costs: If you prevail

💡 Example: $1,200 deposit withheld = $1,200 + $500 penalty + interest + attorney fees = $2,000+

Evidence Needed:
  • Move-out date (lease end, keys returned)
  • Notice of where to send deposit (address provided)
  • No itemized statement received within 21 days
  • Photos of unit condition at move-out
  • Move-in inspection report
How to File:
  1. Draft "Answer and Counterclaim" (if in eviction case) OR file separate lawsuit in conciliation court if under $15,000
  2. Allege violation of § 504B.178
  3. Demand: Deposit + $500 penalty + interest + costs + attorney fees
  4. Attach photos, lease, and move-out notice as exhibits
✅ Success Rate: Very High (90%+) if landlord missed 21-day deadline
2. Retaliation Countersuit
📖 MN Stat. § 504B.285 - Retaliation Prohibited

When to File: Landlord filed eviction within 90 days of you exercising tenant rights (reporting violations, requesting repairs, joining tenant association).

💵 DAMAGES AVAILABLE:
  • 3 Months' Rent as statutory penalty
  • PLUS Attorney Fees & Costs
  • PLUS Actual Damages: Moving costs, storage, higher rent elsewhere
  • Injunctive Relief: Court order stopping eviction

💡 Example: $1,000/month rent × 3 = $3,000 penalty + attorney fees + moving costs

Evidence of Protected Activity (within 90 days before eviction):
  • City/county code complaint filed
  • Written repair requests (emails, letters, texts)
  • Tenant union membership or meeting attendance
  • Report to health department
  • Request for reasonable accommodation (disability)
  • Complaint about discrimination
⚖️ Legal Standard: If protected activity within 90 days, PRESUMPTION of retaliation. Landlord must prove eviction NOT retaliatory.
How to File:
  1. File "Answer and Counterclaim for Retaliation"
  2. Allege protected activity with dates
  3. Allege eviction filed within 90 days
  4. Demand: 3 months' rent + fees + injunction against eviction
  5. Attach: Repair requests, code complaints, photos, inspection reports
⏰ File WITH Answer to eviction (within 7-14 days)
✅ Success Rate: High (70%+) if clear timeline and documentation
3. Breach of Implied Warranty of Habitability
📖 MN Common Law + § 504B.285

When to File: Landlord rented or maintained unit with serious defects affecting habitability.

💵 DAMAGES AVAILABLE:
  • Rent Reduction/Refund: Difference between rent paid and actual value of defective unit
  • Cost of Repairs: If you paid to fix landlord's obligation
  • Alternative Housing Costs: Hotel if uninhabitable
  • Personal Property Damage: Mold ruined clothes, belongings
  • Medical Expenses: Illness from mold, lead, etc.
  • Emotional Distress: If severe conditions
Examples of Habitability Violations:
  • No Heat: Heating system broken in winter (Minnesota!)
  • No Hot Water: Or no water at all
  • Severe Mold: Black mold, respiratory issues
  • Pest Infestation: Rats, mice, bedbugs, roaches
  • Broken Locks: No security for doors/windows
  • Roof Leaks: Water damage, ceiling collapse risk
  • Electrical Hazards: Exposed wiring, fire risk
  • Sewage Backup: Plumbing failure
  • Lead Paint: Especially with children

⚠️ Key Requirement: You MUST have given landlord notice of defects and reasonable time to repair (typically 14 days).

How to Calculate Damages:
  1. Percentage Reduction Method: If unit 50% uninhabitable, refund 50% of rent for period of violation
  2. Example: No heat for 3 months in winter = 75% reduction = $750/month × 3 × 75% = $1,687.50
  3. Add: Out-of-pocket expenses (space heaters, hotel, repairs you paid for)
Evidence to Gather:
  • Photos/videos of conditions (date-stamped)
  • Written repair requests with dates
  • City/county inspection reports
  • Witness statements (neighbors, visitors)
  • Medical records (if illness from conditions)
  • Receipts for repairs, hotels, heaters, etc.
  • Expert testimony (inspector, contractor)
✅ Success Rate: High (75%+) with strong documentation and inspection reports
4. Violation of Tenant Rights Act
📖 MN Stat. § 504B.161 (Entry), § 504B.171 (Utility Shutoff)

When to File: Landlord violated specific statutory tenant rights.

💵 DAMAGES FOR SPECIFIC VIOLATIONS:
  • Illegal Entry (§ 504B.161): Landlord entered without 24-hour notice (except emergency)
    • Actual damages + $100 per violation
    • Attorney fees if sue
  • Utility Shutoff (§ 504B.215): Landlord shut off utilities to force you out
    • 2 months' rent OR actual damages (whichever greater)
    • Attorney fees
    • Injunction restoring utilities
  • Illegal Lockout (§ 504B.375): Landlord changed locks without court order
    • 3 months' rent OR actual damages (whichever greater)
    • Attorney fees
    • Court order for re-entry
Evidence Needed:
  • Illegal Entry: Ring camera footage, texts/emails from landlord, dates/times
  • Utility Shutoff: Utility company records, landlord communications
  • Lockout: Photos of changed locks, inability to enter, belongings locked out
✅ Success Rate: Extremely High (95%+) - These are "slam dunk" cases
⏰ File IMMEDIATELY - Get TRO if ongoing violation
5. Fair Housing Discrimination Claim
📖 Federal Fair Housing Act + MN Human Rights Act (Ch. 363A)

When to File: Eviction or adverse action based on protected characteristic.

💵 DAMAGES AVAILABLE:
  • Actual Damages: Emotional distress, moving costs, higher rent
  • Punitive Damages: Can be substantial if intentional discrimination
  • Attorney Fees & Costs
  • Injunctive Relief: Stop eviction, force accommodation
Protected Characteristics:
  • Race, Color, National Origin
  • Religion
  • Sex (including sexual harassment, pregnancy)
  • Disability (physical or mental)
  • Familial Status (children under 18)
  • Source of Income (Section 8, Social Security)
  • Sexual Orientation (MN law)
  • Marital Status (MN law)
Examples of Discrimination:
  • Refusing reasonable accommodation for disability
  • Evicting family with children but not childless tenants
  • Refusing Section 8 vouchers
  • Sexual harassment by landlord or staff
  • Different rules/enforcement based on race
  • Retaliating for disability-related request
Two Filing Options:
  1. HUD Complaint: File with U.S. Dept of Housing & Urban Development (free, they investigate)
    • Online: www.hud.gov
    • Phone: 800-669-9777
    • Must file within 1 year of discrimination
  2. MN Dept of Human Rights: File with state agency (free)
    • Online: mn.gov/mdhr
    • Phone: 651-539-1100
    • Must file within 1 year
  3. Federal Court Lawsuit: Can file directly in court (need attorney usually)
    • 2-year statute of limitations
    • Can request jury trial
✅ Success Rate: Moderate to High (depends on evidence strength)
⏰ File within 1 year of discriminatory act
6. Fraudulent Misrepresentation
📖 MN Common Law Fraud

When to File: Landlord made false statements to induce you to rent, causing you harm.

💵 DAMAGES AVAILABLE:
  • Out-of-Pocket Losses: Rent paid, moving costs, deposits
  • Benefit of the Bargain: Difference between promised and actual unit
  • Punitive Damages: If fraud was intentional/egregious
Examples of Fraud:
  • Showed photos of renovated unit, gave you unrenovated one
  • Promised parking spot, none actually available
  • Lied about utilities included
  • Concealed major defects (mold behind walls)
  • Promised amenities that don't exist
  • Falsely stated unit was legal (actually illegal rental)
Elements You Must Prove:
  1. Landlord made false statement of fact
  2. Landlord knew it was false
  3. Landlord intended you to rely on it
  4. You actually relied on it
  5. You suffered damages as a result
Evidence Needed:
  • Rental listing (if false claims)
  • Email/text representations
  • Photos showing actual vs. promised condition
  • Witness testimony
  • Receipts for damages/expenses
✅ Success Rate: Moderate (60%) - Hard to prove intent

🎯 Strategic Tips for Countersuits

💡 When to File Counterclaim vs. Separate Lawsuit

File as Counterclaim in Eviction Case When:

  • Your damages can offset rent owed (retaliation, habitability)
  • You want to stop/delay eviction immediately
  • Claims arise from same transaction (the tenancy)
  • Faster resolution (all issues decided together)

File Separate Lawsuit When:

  • Damages exceed eviction court jurisdiction ($50,000+)
  • Complex issues needing jury trial
  • Already evicted and want to sue after
  • Discrimination claim (often better in federal court)

⚖️ Using Counterclaims as Leverage

Negotiation Power:

  • Strong counterclaim = landlord more likely to settle
  • Example: Owe $3,000 rent, but $5,000 retaliation claim = You might walk away even or ahead
  • Landlord fears attorney fee liability if you win
  • Can negotiate "mutual dismissal" - no money, no eviction record
